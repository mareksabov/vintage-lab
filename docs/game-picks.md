# Game picks

A curated "what to actually run" list for the machines in this lab. Not a
complete library — just the titles that defined each era and still hold up.

No media is distributed with this project; see [Where to get them](#where-to-get-them)
at the bottom for legal sources.

## OptiPlex GX — DOS era (1989–1995)

The Pentium II 400 / SB16 profile runs this whole era comfortably — it is in
fact wildly overpowered for it, so expect to use 86Box's CPU throttling for the
oldest titles.

### The two-player night

| Title | Year | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Tunneler** | 1991 | Split-screen tanks digging tunnels through dirt; when you meet, you shoot. Shared keyboard, fuel management, one of the most balanced two-player DOS games ever written. By Joey deVilla. |
| **Dyna Blaster** | 1992 | Bomberman on PC. Best couch multiplayer of the era. |
| **Scorched Earth** | 1991 | "The mother of all games." Artillery duels, absurd weapon shop. |
| **One Must Fall 2097** | 1994 | Robot fighter, shareware legend, superb soundtrack. |

### Essentials

| Title | Year | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Another World** | 1991 | Rotoscoped sci-fi told with zero words. Still looks extraordinary. |
| **Flashback** | 1992 | The spiritual sibling of Prince of Persia — same fluid animation, deeper world. |
| **Prince of Persia 2: The Shadow and the Flame** | 1993 | The obvious next step from PoP 1. |
| **Dune II** | 1992 | Ancestor of every RTS that followed. |
| **X-COM: UFO Defense** | 1994 | The most cult turn-based game ever made. |
| **DOOM** | 1993 | — |
| **The Secret of Monkey Island** | 1990 | Peak adventure-game writing. |
| **Alone in the Dark** | 1992 | First 3D survival horror; Resident Evil's direct ancestor. |
| **Lemmings** | 1991 | — |
| **Sid Meier's Civilization** | 1991 | "One more turn." |

### By genre

**Shooters** — Wolfenstein 3D (1992), Heretic, Hexen, Rise of the Triad, Blood,
Descent (1995, six degrees of freedom, still unique), Duke Nukem 3D (1996).

**Platformers** — Commander Keen (1990), Jazz Jackrabbit (1994),
Prehistorik 2 (1993), Little Big Adventure (1994), Gobliiins (1991).

**Adventures** — Day of the Tentacle (1993), Sam & Max Hit the Road,
Indiana Jones and the Fate of Atlantis (1992), Full Throttle (1995), Loom,
Gabriel Knight (1993), Myst and The 7th Guest (1993).

**Strategy** — Warcraft: Orcs & Humans (1994), Warcraft II (1995),
Command & Conquer (1995), Transport Tycoon (1994), Theme Park (1994),
The Settlers (1993), Master of Orion (1993), Master of Magic (1994),
Panzer General (1994), Heroes of Might & Magic (1995), Colonization (1994).

**RPG** — Ultima VII (1992), Ultima Underworld (1992, the first real 3D dungeon
and the root of the whole immersive-sim genre), System Shock (1994),
Eye of the Beholder, Lands of Lore, Betrayal at Krondor (1993).

**Space & racing** — Wing Commander (1990), X-Wing (1993), TIE Fighter (1994),
Star Control II (1992), Frontier: Elite II (1993), Stunts (1990),
Screamer (1995).

**Also** — Syndicate (1993), Cannon Fodder (1993), Pirates! (1987),
Tyrian (1995), Raptor: Call of the Shadows (1994), Epic Pinball, and
`GORILLA.BAS` / `NIBBLES.BAS` straight out of MS-DOS QBasic.

## OptiPlex GX — Windows 98 era (1996–2001)

**Games** — Half-Life (1998) + Counter-Strike, Quake II (1997), Unreal (1998),
StarCraft + Brood War (1998), Age of Empires II (1999), Diablo II (2000),
Baldur's Gate I/II, Fallout 1 & 2, Planescape: Torment, System Shock 2,
Heroes of Might & Magic III (1999), The Sims (2000), GTA 1 & 2,
Need for Speed III: Hot Pursuit (1998), Grim Fandango (1998),
Worms Armageddon, 3D Pinball: Space Cadet.

**Software, for the atmosphere** — Winamp 2.x, ICQ 99b, mIRC,
Netscape Communicator 4, Windows Commander, WinZip, Nero Burning ROM,
Paint Shop Pro 5, ACDSee, Office 97, Visual Basic 6, DivX 3.11,
Microsoft Plus! themes.

**Hardware caveat:** the shipped profile uses an **S3 ViRGE/DX**, which is a
3D *decelerator*. Anything wanting hardware 3D (Quake III, Unreal, NFS in
accelerated mode) needs 86Box's **3dfx Voodoo** emulation instead — much
heavier on the host. Also note the ~4 GB disk: a full Baldur's Gate II install
alone is over 2 GB, so a second HDD image is worth adding before a spree.

## Czech & Slovak titles

| Title | Year | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Vlak** | 1993 | Miroslav Němeček's train puzzler — Snake, but the tail is the cars you collect. 13 kB, freeware, 50 password-gated levels. Ended up on practically every school PC in the country. Version 2.0 (1995) adds sound, a level editor and F1 solutions. |
| **Dračí historie** | 1995 | Czech point-and-click, hand-drawn, genuinely funny. |
| **Tajemství Oslího ostrova** | 1995 | The other beloved Czech adventure of that year. |
| **7 dní a 7 nocí** | 1994 | Early Czech adventure, rough but iconic. |
| **Fish Fillets** | 1998 | Altar's underwater logic puzzler. Later open-sourced. |
| **Polda** | 1998 | Detective comedy, the series everyone quotes. |
| **Original War** | 2001 | Altar again — RTS/RPG hybrid with time travel, still has a cult following. |
| **Operation Flashpoint** | 2001 | Bohemia's debut. Pushes the OptiPlex profile hard. |

**Tunneler** (above) was Canadian, not Czech — but it spread through CZ/SK
school computer labs so thoroughly that everyone remembers it as one of ours.

## Commodore 64 (1984–1993)

Attach a `.d64` via the `drive8` slot in `machines/c64/machine.toml`, or use the
`autostart` slot to attach and run a `.prg`/`.d64` in one step.

| Title | Year | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **The Last Ninja** | 1987 | Isometric, gorgeous, brutally hard. Sequels 2 and 3 too. |
| **Turrican II** | 1991 | The C64's technical showpiece. Chris Hülsbeck soundtrack. |
| **Impossible Mission** | 1984 | "Another visitor! Stay a while… stay *forever!*" |
| **Boulder Dash** | 1984 | Perfect arcade-puzzle loop. |
| **Paradroid** | 1985 | Andrew Braybrook's droid-hijacking classic; the takeover minigame is genius. |
| **Uridium** | 1986 | Braybrook again — the smoothest scrolling on the machine. |
| **Wizball** | 1987 | Sensible Software at their strangest and best. |
| **IK+** | 1987 | Three-way karate. Still the best fighting game on 8-bit. |
| **The Great Giana Sisters** | 1987 | Pulled from shelves for being too Mario. Legendary music. |
| **Bruce Lee** | 1984 | Two players, one co-operative, one adversarial. |
| **Maniac Mansion** | 1987 | LucasArts' first SCUMM game. |
| **Elite** | 1985 | An entire galaxy in 64 kB. |
| **Project Firestart** | 1989 | Survival horror years before the term existed. |
| **Mayhem in Monsterland** | 1993 | Proof the C64 still had headroom in 1993. |
| **Armalyte** | 1988 | The best shoot-'em-up on the system. |
| **California Games** | 1987 | Epyx's peak; also Summer/Winter Games. |
| **Nebulus** | 1987 | Rotating tower platforming, unlike anything else. |
| **Head over Heels** | 1987 | Isometric puzzling, two characters, one brain. |
| **Defender of the Crown** | 1987 | Cinemaware's showpiece. |
| **Pirates!** | 1987 | Sid Meier's most charming design. |
| **Creatures 2: Torture Trouble** | 1992 | Late-era Apex, ridiculous and brilliant. |
| **Space Taxi** | 1984 | "Hey, taxi!" — the first game with speech everyone remembers. |

Also worth a night on their own: the **SID soundtracks**. Rob Hubbard
(*Monty on the Run*, *Commando*), Martin Galway (*Wizball*, *Rambo*) and
Jeroen Tel (*Cybernoid II*, *Myth*) are the reason half these titles are
remembered.

## VIC-20 (1981–1985)

Different machine, different rules: **5 kB of RAM unexpanded**. Most cult
titles are cartridge releases; several tape/disk games need an **+8K or +16K
memory expansion**, and a game written for an expanded VIC simply will not boot
without it. Check the requirement before blaming the emulator.

| Title | Year | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Omega Race** | 1982 | Regularly voted the best game on the machine. Vector-style arena combat. |
| **Gorf** | 1982 | The finest arcade conversion Commodore shipped. Four distinct stages. |
| **Gridrunner** | 1982 | Jeff Minter's breakout hit for Llamasoft. Hard, fair, unforgiving, superb. |
| **The Sword of Fargoal** | 1982 | Epyx dungeon crawl with randomly generated levels — remarkable depth for 8 kB. |
| **Jelly Monsters** | 1981 | The infamous Pac-Man clone Atari sued off the shelves. |
| **Jupiter Lander** | 1981 | Pure lunar-lander physics, one of the launch cartridges. |
| **Radar Rat Race** | 1982 | Maze chase set to "Three Blind Mice" — nobody forgets that tune. |
| **Choplifter** | 1983 | Broderbund's rescue classic, a genuinely good port. |
| **Miner 2049er** | 1983 | Platform climbing across ten tricky mine levels. |
| **Demon Attack** | 1982 | Imagic's shooter, technically well beyond what the VIC should manage. |
| **Jetpac** | 1983 | Ultimate Play the Game's debut, on VIC before it made them famous. |
| **Lode Runner** | 1983 | Dig, trap, escape. |
| **Donkey Kong** | 1983 | Atarisoft's cartridge — along with **Pole Position** and Parker Brothers' **Frogger** and **Q\*bert**, the arcade cartridges worth owning. |
| **Laser Zone / Matrix** | 1982–83 | More Llamasoft; Minter essentially defined the VIC's shooter library. |
| **Serpentine** | 1982 | Broderbund snake-vs-snake maze game with real tactics. |
| **Adventure Land** | 1982 | Scott Adams' text adventures, on cartridge, in 5 kB. |
| **Perils of Willy** | 1985 | Late-era platformer in the Jet Set Willy mould. |
| **Amok** / **Skramble** / **Arcadia** | 1982–83 | The dependable third-party action trio. |
| **Tooth Invaders** | 1982 | Commodore's dental-hygiene game. Bizarre, and unexpectedly playable. |
| **Avenger** | 1981 | The Space Invaders clone that shipped with the machine. |

The launcher covers this: set `ram = "24k"` in `machines/vic20/machine.toml` for
anything needing expansion, and attach cartridges through the `cart` slot (or
`autostart` to attach and run in one go). See [vice-runtime-notes.md](vice-runtime-notes.md).

## Where to get them

This project ships no games. Legal sources:

- **GOG** — Another World, X-COM, the Ultima and Wing Commander series, the
  LucasArts adventures, Fallout, Baldur's Gate, Heroes III and much of the
  DOS list above, repackaged and licensed.
- **Free from the rights holders** — Doom shareware, Tyrian, Fish Fillets
  (open-sourced), Vlak (freeware from the author), Blizzard's classic
  Diablo and StarCraft releases.
- **[Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/softwarelibrary_msdos_games)** —
  large in-browser DOS and Windows collections.
- **[RetroGames.cz](https://www.retrogames.cz/)** and
  **[OldGames.sk](https://www.oldgames.sk/)** — the Czech/Slovak titles, with
  history and in-browser play.
- **[Gamebase64](https://gb64.com/)** and **[CSDb](https://csdb.dk/)** — the
  C64 archive and demoscene database.
- **[VIC-20 Reloaded](https://files.vic20reloaded.com/)** and
  **[Zimmers.net](http://www.zimmers.net/anonftp/pub/cbm/)** — VIC-20 software.

For anything still commercially sold, buy it — most of these cost less than a
coffee and the re-releases are properly maintained.
